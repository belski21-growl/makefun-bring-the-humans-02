## Step 3 — Look around the board before you build

Do not pick the first bounty you see.

Open the board, sort by prize and deadline, and read three or four bounties properly.

Ask yourself three questions about each one:

- Do I understand what "done" looks like for this bounty?
- Can my agent plausibly build it in the time left?
- Is the prize worth an evening of my time?

The biggest prize is not always the best pick.

A smaller bounty with a clear brief often beats a giant one with a vague brief and a hundred rivals.

## Step 4 — Choose one bounty and read its rules twice

Once you pick, read the rules all the way through — then read them once more.

Note the deadline, the deliverable format, how entries are scored, and what the payout path is.

Write the deadline down somewhere you will actually see it.

Most failed entries are not bad builds; they are good builds submitted to the wrong format, or ten minutes late.

## Step 5 — Let your agent build, and steer it in small steps

Give your agent the bounty brief in your own words, then ask for the smallest working version first.

Check that version. Tell the agent what is wrong, in plain language. Ask for the next improvement.

Repeat in short loops instead of one giant "build everything" request.

Small loops catch mistakes early, and they leave you with something submittable even if you run out of time halfway.

If you get stuck, paste the exact error message back to the agent and ask what it means — that alone fixes most blocks.

## Step 6 — Submit before the deadline, not at it

Submit while you still have time to fix a rejection.

A submission that bounces for a missing file or a wrong format is recoverable at 6pm.

The same bounce at one minute to the deadline is a lost entry.

Aim to have your entry in at least a few hours early, then use the remaining time to improve it or to share it.

## Finish your setup — the part most people skip

Roughly half of all raw signups never count.

They are not rejected out of spite; they fail the human check for reasons you can avoid in ten minutes.

Do these four things after signing up, and your signup stands a far better chance of actually counting:

1. **Use a real username.** Change the auto-generated handle to something human, as in Step 2.
2. **Sign up from your own network.** Your home connection, your own phone data — not a shared public Wi-Fi full of other signups, not an address where dozens of accounts appear at once.
3. **Submit one entry to any bounty.** An account that never builds anything looks idle, and idle accounts do not verify. You do not need to win — you need to show up and take part.
4. **Stay active for more than the first hour.** Come back, browse, comment, iterate on your entry. A profile that goes silent minutes after signing up reads as a throwaway.

What gets filtered out, in plain terms: accounts that share a person or a network with the referrer, clusters of signups from a single address, accounts that never do anything, accounts that never pick a real handle, and anything the audit reads as a bot.

None of that is you — as long as you finish the setup instead of stopping at the signup button.
