## Finish your setup — the part most people skip

This section decides whether your signup counts. A signup that stops here is worth nothing, no matter how many friends you bring. Do all four, and do them on your own connection, from your own device.

1. **Change the auto-generated username.** A fresh account arrives with a random handle like `quick-lynx-56`. Pick a real one. An account still wearing its factory handle looks unfinished, and unfinished accounts are the first ones the verification check doubts.
2. **Sign up from your own network, not a shared one.** Use your own home connection or your own mobile data. Do not sign up from an office cluster, a campus lab, a warnet, or a shared VPN that dozens of strangers also use. Signups that share an IP address with a crowd get grouped together and thrown out as one cluster.
3. **Submit one bounty entry.** Any bounty, any size — even a small one. This is the single clearest proof you are a participant and not a spectator. An account with zero entries is the definition of idle, and idle accounts do not verify.
4. **Stay active past the first hour.** Come back, look at another bounty, read a leaderboard, leave a trace of real use. Accounts that sign up and vanish inside their first hour are treated as if they never arrived.

Work through the four in order. They take about ten minutes, and they are the difference between a signup that counts and one that quietly does not.

What happens next is a 24-hour review hold. The check looks at whether the account did the things above — real handle, own network, one entry, activity past the first hour — and decides. A signup still undecided when the final check closes, 30 hours after the deadline, never counts, so the earlier your friends finish setup, the more time the check has to clear them. Send the link tonight, not on the last day.

### What kills verification

Most raw signups never become verified signups. Right now, only 7 of 2,236 entries in this round have even one verified signup — the rest are still at zero, even though far more people clicked links. Here is what sinks them:

- **Idle accounts.** Signed up, did nothing, never came back. The check calls this idle, and idle never counts.
- **Lurkers.** Accounts that browse but never enter anything and never set a handle. Watching is not participating.
- **Shared IPs.** A whole batch of signups from one address — a VPN exit, a shared office, a bot farm — counts as one cluster, and clusters do not verify.
- **No entry submitted.** The fastest single proof of a real human is one submitted entry. Without it, there is almost nothing for the check to hold on to.
- **Bots and duplicates.** An account that is your own second account, or one that shares your identity, is rejected outright.

The rule is simple: a signup counts when it looks like a person who came to build something. Bring two people who do the four steps above, and you are already in the prize zone.
